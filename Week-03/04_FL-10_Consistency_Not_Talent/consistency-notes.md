# Consistency, Not Talent — Notes

## Consistency

The portfolio identity was built around a small number of decisions made once and reused consistently.

I selected **Playfair Display** for headings and **Inter** for body text. The color system uses a restrained lavender/purple palette supported by near-black and off-white.

The KC monogram provides a simple visual identifier without becoming the main focus of the page.

The purpose of these choices is consistency rather than decoration.

---

## Frame, Not Upstage

The portfolio is meant to showcase the work, so the visual design should support the projects instead of competing with them.

I intentionally kept the identity minimal and calm. This allows project screenshots, case studies, and technical proof to carry the visual weight.

---

## Real Work vs AI-Generated Visuals

For project evidence, I chose real screenshots from my completed projects:

- Advanced Calculator
- Portfolio-Kanak
- AI Internship Landing Page

Real captures were more appropriate because they show the actual interfaces and provide evidence of work completed.

AI-generated visuals were not used as replacements for these project screenshots.

---

## Judgment and Rejection

One of the important decisions in this assignment was recognizing that a visually attractive generated image is not automatically useful.

I rejected generic AI-generated technology/coding artwork when it did not provide meaningful evidence of my work. Although such visuals can look polished, they would add decoration without helping a reviewer understand what I actually built.

The final image set therefore prioritizes relevance, clarity, and proof over visual novelty.

---

## Final Principle

**Make fewer visual decisions, repeat them consistently, and let the work remain the most important thing on the page.**
